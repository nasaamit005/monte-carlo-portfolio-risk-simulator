Research References

These sources provide the theoretical background for portfolio construction, risk measurement, and Monte Carlo simulation. The project is an educational implementation inspired by these concepts; it does not reproduce every method or validation procedure in the papers.

1. Mean-variance portfolio theory

Markowitz, H. (1952). Portfolio Selection. The Journal of Finance, 7(1), 77–91.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1952.tb01525.x

Relevance: Foundational work on portfolio choice and the role of expected returns, variance, and co-movement. This project uses a covariance matrix to represent co-movement, but it uses fixed equal weights rather than optimizing the portfolio.

2. Coherent risk measures

Artzner, P., Delbaen, F., Eber, J.-M., & Heath, D. (1999). Coherent Measures of Risk. Mathematical Finance, 9(3), 203–228.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9965.00068

Relevance: Develops axiomatic properties for risk measures and discusses shortcomings of quantile-based measures. Provides background for interpreting tail-risk estimates carefully.

3. Expected Shortfall

Acerbi, C., & Tasche, D. (2002). On the Coherence of Expected Shortfall. Journal of Banking & Finance, 26(7), 1487–1503.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0378-4266(02)00283-2

Relevance: Examines the coherence properties of Expected Shortfall and distinctions between definitions, particularly for distributions with discontinuities. This project uses a finite-simulation empirical lower-tail average as its ES estimate.

4. Monte Carlo methods in financial engineering

Glasserman, P. (2003). Monte Carlo Methods in Financial Engineering. Springer, Stochastic Modelling and Applied Probability, volume 53.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-387-21617-1

Relevance: Broad reference on Monte Carlo methods in financial engineering, including simulation design, estimation, and variance reduction. The current project implements a baseline simulation and does not implement all techniques covered in the book.

5. VaR model backtesting

Kupiec, P. H. (1995). Techniques for Verifying the Accuracy of Risk Measurement Models. The Journal of Derivatives, 3(2), 73–84.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.3905/jod.1995.407942

Relevance: Background for testing whether realized exceptions are consistent with the exception frequency implied by a VaR model. A Kupiec test is not implemented in the current simulation script.

6. Conditional coverage and VaR exceptions

Christoffersen, P. F. (1998). Evaluating Interval Forecasts. International Economic Review, 39(4), 841–862.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/2527341

Relevance: Useful foundation for evaluating interval forecasts and the independence of forecast violations. It motivates future out-of-sample risk validation, which is not yet implemented here.

7. ARCH effects and volatility clustering

Engle, R. F. (1982). Autoregressive Conditional Heteroscedasticity with Estimates of the Variance of United Kingdom Inflation. Econometrica, 50(4), 987–1007.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/1912773

Relevance: Foundational ARCH paper. It motivates future extensions that model time-varying volatility rather than assuming constant historical covariance throughout the simulated horizon.

Academic integrity note

Cite the original sources when describing the theoretical background. Clearly distinguish published research from this repository's implementation, assumptions, outputs, and limitations. Do not claim the project implements an entire paper when it only uses a related concept.
