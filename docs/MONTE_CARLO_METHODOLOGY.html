Monte Carlo Portfolio Risk Simulator — Methodology

Objective

Estimate a distribution of hypothetical terminal portfolio values and downside risk measures using Monte Carlo simulation calibrated to historical returns.

Portfolio

Five assets are held at equal weights: AAPL, AMZN, GOOGL, MSFT, and NVDA. Weights are reset to equal weights every simulated trading day. The S&P 500 benchmark is not included as a holding in this simulation.

Data and return transformations

Given adjusted or prepared prices (P_{i,t}), calculate log returns:

[ r_{i,t} = \ln(P_{i,t}/P_{i,t-1}) ]

Estimate the sample mean vector (\hat{\mu}) and covariance matrix (\hat{\Sigma}) from the observed daily log-return series.

Monte Carlo model

For every simulation and day, generate correlated asset log returns:

[ \mathbf{r}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\hat{\mu},\hat{\Sigma}) ]

Convert them to simple returns:

[ R_{i,t} = e^{r_{i,t}}-1 ]

Calculate daily equal-weight portfolio returns and compound wealth:

[ R_{p,t} = \sum_i w_i R_{i,t}, \qquad V_T = V_0 \prod_{t=1}^{T}(1+R_{p,t}) ]

The covariance matrix is symmetrized and its eigenvalues are floored at a small positive value for numerical stability before sampling.

Risk metrics

For terminal simple return (R_T=V_T/V_0-1), at confidence level (c):

[ q_{1-c}=Q_{1-c}(R_T) ]

The signed VaR loss amount is (-V_0 q_{1-c}). Expected Shortfall is estimated as the negative initial-capital-scaled mean of terminal returns at or below the lower-tail quantile.

Probability of loss is the proportion of simulations with (V_T<V_0). Terminal value percentiles summarize the modeled distribution, not guaranteed future bounds.

Important distinctions

  • The model simulates a joint distribution of returns; it does not predict a specific market path.
  • Correlation is estimated from the historical covariance matrix, but may not remain stable during stress.
  • The Gaussian assumption can understate fat tails and extreme joint events.
  • Historical mean and covariance estimates are uncertain.
  • No transaction costs, taxes, slippage, or liquidity constraints are included.
  • The output is not investment advice or a validated production risk model.

Suggested validation

  1. Compare simulated and historical marginal return distributions.
  2. Compare model VaR with realized out-of-sample losses.
  3. Perform VaR exception counting and independence/conditional coverage tests.
  4. Test block bootstrap and Student-t alternatives.
  5. Add volatility models and explicit stress scenarios.
  6. Compare results under different estimation windows and rebalancing schedules.
